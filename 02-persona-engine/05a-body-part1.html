Personas that are sized, scored, and provable

Not a workshop exercise, and not five generic archetypes. Every persona comes out of a clustering process run against your real customers -- and there are usually a lot more of them than you'd expect.

The problem with a handful of personas

Your persona deck lists five generic customer types. Your CRM has enough real behavioral variety to support fifty distinct, addressable segments -- and nobody's built them, because the tools available only ever hand you a handful.

Most "persona builders" produce three to five broad-strokes profiles because that's what a workshop has time to name and a slide deck has room to hold. That ceiling isn't a data limitation -- it's a tooling limitation. A mid-sized customer file easily contains dozens of statistically distinct behavioral groups, each addressable with different messaging, sitting unused.

How it actually works

Fan out. Cluster. Converge.

Every customer record fans out as its own data point. Similar records pull together by real behavioral similarity, not a guess. Each resulting group converges into one addressable persona -- and there are always several, never just one.

1. Fan out

Every order, click, and email open becomes its own record -- nothing is pre-grouped or assumed.

2. Cluster

Customers who behave alike pull together, and the groupings are validated for real behavioral separation.

3. Converge

Each group large enough to act on becomes one named, addressable persona.
