Three disciplines that rarely sit together

Data science knows how to cluster but not what marketers need. Analytics knows what happened but not what to write. Marketing knows the message but not the math. Most persona work fails in the gaps between those three. This model was built by someone who had to operate in all three at once, for decades.

Finding the gaps, then filling them

Every persona engagement over those years surfaced the same recurring failures. Segments that were statistically distinct but too small to build a campaign around. Personas that looked convincing in a deck and had no traceable connection to the customer file. Groupings that separated cleanly on demographics and not at all on purchase behavior. The same person counted three times because nobody resolved identity first. Decks that were accurate the week they shipped and obsolete two quarters later.

None of those are exotic problems. They're the ordinary ways persona work goes wrong, and they repeat across companies and categories because the process is manual and nobody systematizes the lessons. After enough repetitions, the fixes stopped being one-off patches and became a method — a set of standards about what a persona has to clear before anyone writes a word against it.

That method is what Triangul8 automates. Not a secret algorithm — a set of hard-won decisions about how to do this correctly, encoded so it runs consistently on your data instead of depending on who happens to be in the room.
