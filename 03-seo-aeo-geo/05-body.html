SEO / AEO / GEO FAQ

Common questions

What is the difference between SEO, AEO, and GEO?

SEO optimizes for ranking in classic search results. AEO (answer engine optimization) targets direct-answer features like featured snippets. GEO (generative engine optimization) targets being cited inside AI-generated answers from tools like ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews. They overlap heavily — the same structural clarity that earns a snippet tends to earn a citation — but GEO puts more weight on being distinctive and demonstrably useful on a specific question.

Can one page be optimized for both search engines and AI engines?

Yes, and it should be. Both reward the same fundamentals: a clear question being answered, a direct response near the top, specific and verifiable detail, and clean structure. The divergence is in emphasis — classic search still rewards topical depth and authority signals, while answer engines reward distinctiveness and extractability. Triangul8 writes for both in a single pass rather than producing two versions.

How much content do I need to see results?

Less than most teams assume, if it's targeted. A small set of pages written for a verified persona and structured for extraction typically outperforms a large set of keyword variants. The volume that matters is coverage of distinct questions your buyers actually ask, not raw page count.

Will AI-generated content hurt my rankings?

Search engines penalize unhelpful content, not content that involved AI. The risk is generic output at scale with no clear audience. Because every page here is written for a specific, sized persona and scored before it ships, the failure mode that gets sites penalized — mass-produced near-duplicate pages — is what the system is built to avoid.

How do you measure AI answer engine visibility?

Through citation tracking across the major answer engines, monitored alongside classic rankings. The practical signal is whether your brand appears as a cited source when someone asks a question you should own. That gets scored and tracked the same way rankings do.

Does this work for product and category pages?

That's the highest-volume use case. Product detail and category pages are usually the largest page count a retailer owns and the most neglected, because writing them by hand at catalog scale is impossible. They're also closest to the transaction, which makes them the highest-leverage pages to get right.
