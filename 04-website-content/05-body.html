On-Page Content

Landing pages, category pages, and product pages, written for the specific persona landing on them.

The problem

Your website is the one channel every paid and organic effort eventually points to -- and it's usually the one built with the least persona awareness of any of them.

Landing pages, category pages, and product pages typically get built once by a design or dev team and then left mostly static, while every other channel -- ads, email, social -- iterates constantly. The result is a website that's often the weakest link in an otherwise persona-aware content strategy.

What Triangul8 does

On-Page Content writes landing pages, category pages, and product detail content for a specific persona, using the same brief and brand-check process as every other channel, and ships it as a draft into your CMS. It also powers the audit workflow: pick an existing, underperforming page, see it scored against the category's best-in-class through the eyes of the persona it's meant to serve, and turn the resulting gap directly into a fix.

The problem

The pages closest to the sale are the ones nobody writes

  • Product pages are the largest page count you own. Thousands to hundreds of thousands of URLs, and the only realistic options have been templated copy, manufacturer descriptions every competitor also uses, or leaving them thin.
  • One voice for every visitor. A cautious first-time buyer and a returning loyalist land on the same category page and read identical copy, despite needing completely different things to convert.
  • Writing capacity caps growth. Every new category, seasonal push, or marketplace expansion needs more writer hours, so content volume moves with headcount instead of with the business.
  • Near-duplicates compete with each other. Templated pages at scale split authority and make the pages that deserve to rank harder to surface.

Website Content FAQ

Common questions

How many product pages can Triangul8 write?

Volume is measured in pooled words rather than page count, so the practical ceiling depends on your package and page length. A typical product page runs a few hundred words, which means even mid-tier allowances cover thousands of pages a year. Catalog scale is the use case the system was built for.

Will the content be unique for every product?

Yes. Each page is generated against that product's own attributes and the persona most likely to view it, rather than filled into a shared template. That's the difference between content that adds coverage and content that creates near-duplicates competing with each other.

Does it work with my CMS?

Approved content publishes as drafts into your existing CMS or commerce platform — Shopify and other major platforms are supported directly, and flat-file export works for anything else. Your team reviews and publishes from the tools they already use.

How does it know my brand voice?

Brand voice, banned terms, mandatory claims, and reading level are loaded during implementation and enforced as pass-or-fail checks on every draft. Consistency at volume is easier for a system than for a rotating group of writers.

Can it rewrite existing pages, not just create new ones?

Yes, and this is often the fastest win. Thin, templated, or near-duplicate pages can be regenerated for a specific persona, and pages competing for the same intent can be consolidated. Fixing what's already live frequently outperforms publishing more.

What about marketplace listings?

Amazon A+ content, Walmart listings, and other marketplace surfaces are generated against each platform's own content rules while holding the same positioning as your owned site.
